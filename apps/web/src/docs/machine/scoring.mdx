# Scoring

## Points for a round

A round is one battle. It ends when one bot or none is alive, or at the cycle cap (100,000 cycles in the arena by default). Each bot alive at the end is a survivor. With N bots in the round and S survivors, each survivor scores `floor((N * N - 1) / S)`, and each dead bot scores 0. If every bot dies, no one scores. This is the multi-warrior scoring of pMARS, the standard Core War simulator.

| bots (N) | 1 survivor | 2 | 3 | 4 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | 1 | | | |
| 3 | 8 | 4 | 2 | | |
| 4 | 15 | 7 | 5 | 3 | |
| 8 | 63 | 31 | 21 | 15 | 7 |

In a duel that is the classic 3 for a win, 1 for a tie, 0 for a loss. A bot that lives to the cycle cap is a survivor: a tie is worth points, and a bot that cannot win can still play not to lose.

## Why it is shaped this way

Each round hands out about N × N - 1 points in all, split evenly among the survivors. So a kill pays by leaving fewer bots to share with, and survival pays every round: in an 8-bot melee, one of 4 survivors scores 15, and a dead bot 0. A bot that is hard to kill scores well without killing much. The imp is the extreme case: it almost never kills, and it seldom dies.

## Points for a match

A match is K rounds with the seeds `seed`, `seed + 1`, …, `seed + K - 1`, and the bot order rotated each round (see [placement](/docs/machine/placement#rounds-and-matches)). A bot's match score is the sum of its round points. A duel of 10 rounds with 6 wins, 3 ties, and 1 loss scores 6 × 3 + 3 × 1 = 21.

The editor's `test vs` counts a match as wins, ties, and losses, and the arena's **standings** show the running points of a match of several rounds. Tournaments rank by these points too.

## A lone bot

A bot alone in the core has no one to outlive, so its battle runs until it dies or the cycle cap. That is how the debugger runs a bot with no opponents, and how you find out that it dies on its own.

See also: [death](/docs/machine/death), for what ends a bot before the cap.
