# Hills

## King of the hill

A hill is a standing field of bots: a round robin that never ends. A new bot, the **challenger**, fights every entry on the hill, one match each. Then the whole field, the hill and the challenger, is ranked on equal terms, and if the field is over the hill's size, the lowest bot goes. The top bot is the **king**.

<Note>

Hills run on the server. [/hills](/hills) lists them, with the roster bots on them from the start. Sign in, keep a bot in your account (the editor's `save`), and `submit` it from a hill's page. The rules below are the ones `@asmbots/tourney` runs, and the server runs the same code. To see how a bot would fare on a hill, `challenge` an entry in the arena: the arena plays the hill's own match. An entry whose source is private fights on the server only.

</Note>

## How a challenge scores

1. The challenger fights each entry: one match of the hill's rounds, under the hill's rules.
2. Each bot's score is the sum of its match points against every other bot in the field. The entries' old matches with each other are kept, not played again, so a challenge plays one match per entry.
3. The field ranks by score. Level scores rank by **age**, the count of challenges a bot has stayed on the hill through: the older bot ranks higher. Level again, the old board order holds, with the challenger after every entry.
4. If the field is over the hill's size, the lowest bot is pushed off, and its matches leave everyone's scores.
5. Each entry that stays ages by one.

So a challenger stays only if it scores more than the lowest entry, and a tie with it is not enough.

A challenger with the same bytes as an entry replaces that entry: the old one and its matches go, and the challenger fights the rest and keeps the old entry's age. The server's hills refuse such a submission, since it would take another bot's place and age: change the code to challenge it. A version already on the hill is refused too.

## The hills at launch

| hill | entries | rules |
|---|---|---|
| `main` | 32 | duels of 10 rounds, 80,000 cycles a round, bots up to 512 bytes |
| `tiny` | 16 | duels of 50,000 cycles a round, bots up to 256 bytes |
| `melee` | 8 | 8-bot melees of 10 rounds, scored as a melee |

The `hill rules` preset of the arena and the tournament form is `main`'s rules: 10 rounds of 80,000 cycles.

## The hill page

A hill's page shows its standings (rank, bot, author, score, rating ± RD, W/T/L, age), king first, the king's card, the recent submissions with their deltas (`+3 rank`: the places a bot gained over its best version before), and the latest matches, each one a `watch` link. `challenge` on a row fights one of your bots against that entry in the arena, under the hill's rules and seed, yours first: the same match the server would play.

With sign-in, `submit` enters a version of one of your account's bots. The server uses the bytes it assembled when you saved that version, so it never takes bytes on trust, and fights the entries one match at a time: "fighting 24 of 32". The result card gives the rank, or, if the bot does not make the hill, its score, the score it had to beat, and its closest fight. You can have one submission running on a hill at a time, and make five an hour. The `melee` hill takes no submissions yet.

Every hill match is a match like any other, with seeds, an order, and result hashes, so you can watch any of them in the arena and check it (see [verification](/docs/tournaments/verification)).

## Strategy on a hill

A hill rewards points taken from every entry, not match wins; [hill meta](/docs/strategy/hill-meta) works through a hill of the roster's fighters.
